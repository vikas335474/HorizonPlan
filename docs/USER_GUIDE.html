A guide for advisors

What everyone can do, and what every feature is for.

HorizonPlan is the planning and client-conversation layer for a modern advisory practice: model a client's retirement properly, show them the risk a spreadsheet hides, and keep a clean record of every plan and every change. This guide walks through who can see and do what (by role), then gives a plain-language tour of every major feature and the value it delivers. No technical background needed.

Who can do what — the five roles

Everyone signs in to the same app, but what they see depends on their role. Roles are enforced on the server for every single action — hiding a button is never the only thing standing between a person and data they shouldn't reach. A firm's data is also completely isolated from every other firm's.

Platform

Super Admin

super_admin
The HorizonPlan platform operator (not one of the advisory firms). Sees across all firms.

Can do

  • Create and manage advisory firms; add the first advisor to a firm
  • Set a firm's compliance mode (advisory vs distribution) — this is a Super-Admin-only control
  • Approve the global strategy templates every firm can draw from
  • Turn platform-wide settings on/off: mandatory 2FA, demo mode, self-serve signup
  • Reset the public demo data

Good to know

  • Passes every role check, but still can't approve another firm's own templates — that stays with the firm
  • Exists to run the platform, not to advise clients day to day
Firm

Firm Admin

advisor · firm_admin
The principal / owner of an advisory firm. Full advisor abilities, plus firm-level control.

Can do

  • Everything a senior advisor can do (below)
  • Set the firm's white-label branding — name, logo, colour — across every client view
  • Approve the firm's own strategy templates, risk questionnaire, and plans

Cannot do

  • Change the firm's compliance mode (advisory_mode) — that's Super-Admin-only, on purpose
Firm

Senior Advisor

advisor · sr_advisor
A senior advisor who can sign off on advice. The default level for existing advisor accounts.

Can do

  • Onboard clients and build their goals, portfolios, cash-flow and risk profiles
  • Run projections, what-if scenarios, and present in Meeting Mode
  • Approve plans a junior advisor submitted for review
  • Approve the firm's strategy templates and risk questionnaire
  • Set each client's review reminder cadence

Good to know

  • The plan-review sign-off role — the second pair of eyes before advice reaches a client
Firm

Junior Advisor

advisor · jr_advisor
A junior advisor who prepares the work but doesn't have final sign-off.

Can do

  • Onboard clients; build and edit goals, portfolios, cash-flow, risk profiles
  • Create and fork strategy templates; draft the risk questionnaire
  • Submit a plan for a senior advisor's review

Cannot do

  • Approve plans, templates, or the risk questionnaire — those go to a senior advisor / firm admin
Client

Client

client
The end investor, with their own self-service login into just their own plan.

Can do

  • See their own goals and readiness score
  • Move the what-if sliders to explore scenarios themselves
  • View their portfolio, cash flow, and risk band — read-only
  • Open their own plan in Meeting Mode or as a printable report

Never sees

  • Other clients, or anything firm-wide
  • Advisor-only framing (e.g. the "is this fundable from cash flow?" judgement)

Feature tour

Every major feature, in plain language: what it is, who uses it, and the value it delivers. Grouped into the parts of the job — the plan and its risk, the money and the household, the advice and its governance, the meeting itself, and the trust that holds it together.

▸ Planning & risk

Plan

Goals, modelled properly

Advisors build; clients view. Each client can have several goals — retirement, a child's education, a home — each with its own numbers. Retirement goals model the full lifecycle: the accumulation years (a monthly SIP that can step up each year) flowing straight into the decumulation (drawdown) years, as one continuous picture. You can also split the corpus into liquid and locked buckets (EPF/NPS/PPF) so the plan spends the liquid money first.

Assumptions built for Indian markets and inflation, not a generic overseas calculator — and the accumulation and drawdown phases join up instead of living in two separate spreadsheets.

Explore

What-if scenarios

Advisors and clients. Spin up a "what if" variant of a goal — retire two years later, save a bit more, assume a lower return — without disturbing the base plan. Change an assumption on the base plan and every non-customised scenario keeps up automatically; the ones you've deliberately tweaked stay frozen.

Answer "what would it take?" live in the meeting, safely, without ever losing the original plan.

See the risk

Readiness score, sequence risk & historical replay

Advisors and clients. A single Retirement Readiness Score (0–100) a client can hold onto. A sequence-of-returns chart that shows how the same average return plays out very differently if the bad years come early. And a historical replay — "what if you'd retired in a specific real market year?" — using actual market history.

Makes the risk that averages hide visible and memorable — the difference between a client nodding along and a client who genuinely understands their plan.

▸ Money & households

Portfolio

Portfolio ledger & daily price sync

Advisors build; clients view read-only. A running balance sheet per client — assets and liabilities, liquid vs. locked, net worth computed for you. Import holdings straight from a CAS / MFCentral CSV. Mutual-fund holdings are refreshed daily from cached NAVs, and a "Refresh prices" button re-values them on demand.

The client's real financial picture sits alongside the plan — no re-keying, no separate spreadsheet. (It tracks values from cached prices; it is not a live RTA folio feed.)

Cash flow

Cash-flow view & the fundability gap

Advisors build; clients see a simpler read-only version. List a client's income and expenses (monthly or annual) and HorizonPlan sums them into one monthly surplus. For the advisor, it compares that surplus against the plan's total SIPs and answers the blunt question: is this plan actually fundable from the client's cash flow? The client's view shows the cash flow without that advisor-only framing.

Catches the plan that looks great on paper but the client can't actually afford — before it's promised.

Household

Household / family planning

Advisors. Group a family into a household and see their goals, corpus and readiness combined, plus a cash-flow roll-up summed across every member — instead of planning each spouse or parent one at a time.

Families retire as a unit; this plans them as one.

▸ Advice & governance

Risk profiler

Your firm's own risk questionnaire

Advisors author; clients answer & see their band. The firm supplies its own questions and scoring — HorizonPlan never authors the questionnaire for you. Once a senior advisor approves the set, a client's answers produce a risk band, and only then does a suggested return assumption surface. Clients can see their own band read-only.

A risk process that's genuinely yours and compliant, not a black box — and a suggested number never appears until a principal has signed off on the questions behind it.

Templates

Strategy templates, approval-gated

Advisors; global ones curated by the platform. A library of model asset allocations and return assumptions — global templates every firm can draw from, plus your firm's own and forked variants. A template can't be applied to a real client's plan until it's approved; applying one writes the assumption into the goal and records exactly where it came from.

Consistent, defensible advice across the firm — with a deliberate sign-off step before any model touches a real client.

Oversight

Jr → Sr plan-approval workflow

Junior advisors submit; senior advisors / firm admins approve. When switched on for a firm, a plan a junior advisor changes enters a review queue. A senior advisor approves it or requests changes, with notes. Every advice edit can move a plan into review automatically.

A real second-pair-of-eyes control before advice reaches a client — the oversight a firm principal needs, built in rather than bolted on.

Follow-through

Scheduled review reminders

Advisors set the cadence. Put a client on a quarterly or annual review cadence and HorizonPlan emails a reminder when it's due. Off by default — no client is enrolled without you choosing it.

The plan doesn't go stale in a drawer; the review conversation actually happens.

The record

Full audit trail

Advisors & firm admins. Every change to a plan or scenario is logged — who changed what, and when — visible per goal and as a firm-wide activity feed. Read-only; you can't quietly rewrite history.

A clean, defensible record for compliance and for the "why did we change this?" conversation a year later.

▸ The meeting & the client

Present

Meeting Mode

Advisors & clients. A full-screen, presentation-ready walkthrough of a plan with a live timeline slider — built for the room, not a screen-share of a spreadsheet.

The retirement conversation your client actually remembers.

Leave-behind

Printable client report

Advisors & clients. A clean, branded, printable summary of the plan — export to PDF straight from the browser.

A professional leave-behind that carries the firm's name, not a screenshot.

Self-service

Client login

Clients. Clients sign in to see just their own goals, move the sliders, and view their portfolio, cash flow and risk band — read-only, anytime.

Engagement between meetings, without exposing anything firm-wide.

Brand

White-label branding

Firm admins set it. The firm's name, logo and colour across every client-facing view.

Clients experience your firm, not a generic tool.

▸ Security & trust

Trust

Two-factor, isolation & disclosures

Everyone. Sign in with an authenticator app or Google as a second factor. Every firm's data is strictly isolated from every other firm's. And the right compliance disclosure — matching whether the firm operates in advisory or distribution mode — renders on every client-facing plan view, right next to the projection and the withdrawal-rate slider.

The basics that let a client, and a regulator, trust what they're looking at.

A note on the compliance disclosure

The disclosure wording is driven by the firm's compliance mode, which only a Super Admin can set. It always appears on client-facing plan views — advisors and clients can read it, but no one inside a firm can quietly change or remove it.

What HorizonPlan is — and isn't

What it is

The planning and client-relationship layer: deep on the retirement story, the meeting, the follow-through, and the record — the part transaction platforms treat as a bolt-on. Make the planning and the client experience genuinely good.

What it isn't

Not a transaction back-office. It doesn't place orders, do Video KYC, or reconcile commissions — it focuses on advice, not execution. We're straight about that boundary on purpose. Keep your back-office; let HorizonPlan own the planning.

Glossary

Accumulation — the saving-up years before retirement (e.g. a monthly SIP).   Decumulation / drawdown — the years after retirement, living off the corpus.

Corpus — the total pot of retirement savings.   Liquid vs. locked — money you can spend now vs. tied up in EPF/NPS/PPF until a later age.

Withdrawal rate — the % of the corpus drawn each year in retirement.   Sequence-of-returns risk — the danger that a bad run of returns early in retirement does lasting damage, even at the same average.

Readiness score — a single 0–100 measure of how well a plan holds up.   What-if scenario — a saved variation of a goal with different assumptions.

Advisory vs. distribution mode — a firm's compliance posture, which sets the disclosure wording clients see.   SIP — a Systematic Investment Plan, i.e. a regular monthly investment.
